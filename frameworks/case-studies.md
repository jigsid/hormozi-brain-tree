## Watch These 51 Minutes if You Want to Grow Your Business in 2026 (Dave Ramsey Interview)
`-HJg4TYBgtI` · https://youtu.be/-HJg4TYBgtI

*Hormozi interviews Dave Ramsey about how Ramsey Solutions is structured - 14 profit centers built over 35 years on 10% of ideas, in-person only, no equity and no exit - and Ramsey's most expensive failure: a debit card that was killed after several million dollars because the fraud risk was unbounded.*

### Case study

**Business.** Ramsey Solutions' failed product: a Ramsey-branded debit card (third-party case study told by Dave Ramsey, not Hormozi's own business)

**Starting state.** Several million dollars invested into building a debit card that was on-brand for Ramsey, with about 10,000 people in the beta. He says they did not know what they did not know about banking regulations.

**Diagnosis.** The product was not the problem - the risk was. Outside fraud bots began hitting the card, they had assumed the bank's software would catch it, and their contract made Ramsey responsible for the overdrafts being created. Ramsey's framing: the several million already spent was small compared to how bad the downside could get, because the losses could be infinite.

**Intervention.**

- Compare the sunk cost (several million) against the unbounded downside (contractual liability for overdrafts)
- Kill the product outright - 'we blew it up' - rather than protect the money already spent
- Accept the write-off and move on

**Result.** The card was killed. Ramsey adds that the bank they had been working with later 'went sideways and was in the news and blew up', which he reads as having exited the risk early.

**Numbers.**

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| Money invested in the failed debit card | **several millions of dollars into building a debit card** |
| The risk that ended it | **contractual responsibility for fraud-created overdrafts -…** |

> **Transferable pattern.** Judge a failing project on the downside, not the sunk cost. When a test shows that losses could be unbounded - here, contractual liability for fraud losses that the partner's software did not catch - kill it even if several million dollars are already in, because the money spent is small next to the worst case. Ramsey's secondary point: the partner he exited later blew up, which is what protecting the downside looks like in hindsight.

**Where this corrects or extends the written summaries.**

- The video contains a full third-party failure case with numbers: several million dollars into a Ramsey debit card, ~10,000 beta users, killed because the contract made Ramsey liable for fraud-created overdrafts (unbounded downside), and the partner bank later failed.
- Ramsey gives a concrete idea hit rate and cadence - about 14 profit centers over ~35 years (one per ~2.5 years) built on about 10% of ideas, with 80-90% of new division leaders promoted from within - which is the operating system behind the 'test tube' advice.
- The five stages are given with names (treadmill, pathfinder, trailblazer, legacy - he skips one in the telling) and explicitly not mapped to revenue bands; the HVAC example (20 trucks, 70 employees) is used to show a business can still be stuck in an early stage.

## I Confronted a Stranger With a Failing Business...
`6Fg4VXjRphQ` · https://youtu.be/6Fg4VXjRphQ

*Teardown of a music-industry law firm losing $100K a year on $300K of revenue: kill the unprofitable software, point every traffic source at the channel that already works (Instagram DMs), stop billing by the hour, and sell the outcome at $5,800.*

### Case study

**Business.** A law firm for Latin-music artists and entrepreneurs (owner introduced as 'Alexi Omar' in the captions) with three units: legal services, education (courses/community), and a contract-template software product

**Starting state.** Last 12 months: $300,000 revenue and a $100,000 loss, with about 6 months of cash left. Services $131,000 revenue / ~$10,000 profit; education $131,000 revenue; software $40,000 revenue while burning $7,000/month (~$150,000/year cost). Distribution: 90,000 YouTube subscribers, 23,000 Instagram followers, 800 new Instagram followers a month, a website doing 7,000 visits and 150 leads (2% opt-in), 9 of 150 buying a $500 course plus $500 upsell. Services were billed at $250/hour (about 524 hours…

**Diagnosis.** Focus and monetization, not demand. He was running three businesses and splitting one source of eyeballs across three destinations, two of which lose money - the education is effectively a charity and the software costs $150,000 a year to make $40,000. Meanwhile all the actual revenue came from Instagram DMs, a channel he never promoted. The profitable unit was underpriced and hourly-billed, which both caps revenue and misaligns incentives, and he had no qualification, no video sales letter and no price anchor.

**Intervention.**

- Kill the software (drawn as a red X): either sell it to the interested B2B buyers at cost or shut it down; the education stays but is reframed as a charity/long-term lead nurture, not a profit centre
- Point every traffic source at the one channel that already works - 'DM me on Instagram' - in four places: a 30-second CTA at the 30% mark of every video, the description above the fold, a pinned comment, and the profile
- Make the DM the funnel: have them book a call without paying; the free 'consultation' is explicitly a sales call, preceded by a 5-7 minute VSSL that sets expectations and shows proof
- Qualify before the landing page on BANT plus income (not Spotify stats): 10 million artists on Spotify but only 1,000-2,000 are professional
- Stop billing hourly: sell the outcome at $5,800, with a $25,000 no-commission alternative as the price anchor, plus the 5% on deals
- Downsell unqualified or lower-intent leads to the education product at $2,000-$3,000 instead of $500-$500 - a phone call typically triples the price of the same thing
- DM every new follower with 'are you here for free content or do you want help?' - 800 new followers a month, close 5%, that is 40 extra sales a month at $6K = $240,000
- Give the book to unqualified leads as an elegant long-term relationship play; use the education community (Skool rather than Kajabi) to generate free onboarding calls
- Enforce delivery economics: at least 80% gross margin, meaning cost under about $1,100 on a $5,800 sale (under 11 hours at $100/hour)
- Price speaking engagements at opportunity cost: 3 sales a day at $6,000 over 2 days = $36,000 plus travel, because demand is not the constraint

**Result.** No after-numbers - the video ends with the owner agreeing to the plan ('Will you do this? Yes.'). Hormozi's projections: $240,000 a month from the new-follower DM play alone, $18,000-$50,000 a day with three sellers at a $6,000 offer, and the next constraint becoming supply (running out of lawyers) rather than demand. He also says he does not know if the client will reach $1M a month from this.

**Numbers.**

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting state | **$300,000 revenue, -$100,000 profit, 6 months of cash** |
| Software drain | **costs $150,000 a year and makes you 40 [thousand]** |
| Price changed to | **$5,800 outcome-based price** |
| Projected result of the DM play | **40 more sales a month at 6K, about 240 [thousand]** |
| Margin rule | **80% gross margin minimum, cost cap ~$1,100 per $5,800 sale** |

> **Transferable pattern.** Run the audit in this order: (1) kill or sell the unit that loses money - do not fund a fantasy while the cash runs out; (2) find where the money actually comes from today (here, unpromoted Instagram DMs) and point every existing traffic source at it instead of fixing the channel that never worked; (3) qualify hard before the sales call (budget, authority, need, timing) so the seller's time is only spent on people who can buy; (4) replace hourly pricing with outcome pricing and set a price anchor above it ($25,000 vs $5,800); (5) downsell everyone who does not qualify to a cheaper product at…

**Where this corrects or extends the written summaries.**

- The diagnosis is arithmetic and checkable: the software costs $150,000 a year to make $40,000, the education is break-even once separated, and the only profitable unit ($131,000 services, ~$10,000 profit) was the one being neglected - that is what turns a $100,000 loss into a fixable problem.
- The pricing move is specific: from $250/hour to a $5,800 outcome retainer with a $25,000 no-commission anchor, plus a repriced $2,000-$3,000 education downsell, on the rule that a phone call typically triples the price of a checkout-page sale.

- Speaking-fee math is given as opportunity cost: 3 sales/day at $6,000 over 2 days equals a $36,000 minimum fee plus travel.

## The first 5 hires EVERY entrepreneur should make...or go broke...
`7NqsFh5ej0w` · https://youtu.be/7NqsFh5ej0w

*Hire in reverse order of the value you personally provide (and the market cost to replace it) - admin, customer support, sales/setter, marketing assist, fractional bookkeeper, then IT - while choosing which type of debt you incur as you grow.*

### Case study

**Business.** His own portfolio companies (Acquisition.com, gym/fitness businesses) used as the reference case for sequencing the first hires

**Starting state.** Early-stage business where the founder is doing everything; administrative work, support, sales, marketing and finance all sit on the owner

**Diagnosis.** The constraint is the founder's own time and the debt the business accrues while growing. Two debts matter: replacing skills in the wrong order (expensive skills first) and letting CRM, bookkeeping, people and management gaps build up, which then have to be paid back with interest at larger scale.

**Intervention.**

- Hire in reverse order of the value you provide and the marketplace cost to replace it: (1) administrative help, (2) customer success/support, (3) first salesperson or setter, (4) marketing assist/prospecting or videographer, (5) fractional bookkeeper, and often a sixth IT/tech person
- Do not take these as hard and fast rules - shift sales earlier if sales is not your strong suit, later if you are a killer closer
- Choose which debt to incur: use financial debt (investment) to incur less operational, talent, CRM and management debt
- Ascend each early hire over time into a director/manager with a team hanging off them

**Result.** He claims the strategy of choosing financial debt over other debts is why each subsequent company grew bigger, faster and stronger than the ones before it, because they could skip the stages (new CRM, fixed financials, better management) that normally set a business back a quarter to a year.

**Numbers.**

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue below which a fractional bookkeeper is sufficient | **$100,000 a month** |
| Annual revenue of Acquisition.com's portfolio | **$85 million a year** |

> **Transferable pattern.** Sequence your first hires by dollars-per-hour math, not by org chart: replace the cheapest, lowest-value-per-hour work first (admin, support), keep selling and marketing yourself while those are your highest-value hours, and take fractional help (bookkeeper, lawyer, tech) rather than in-house until scale justifies it. Then explicitly pick which debt to incur - paying for infrastructure and talent early (financial debt) is cheaper than paying back CRM, financial and management debt with interest later.

**Where this corrects or extends the written summaries.**

- Written summaries list the five hires but omit the two thresholds he gives here: the ordering rule is explicitly reverse of value-per-hour, and the fractional bookkeeper is stated as sufficient below $100,000/month of revenue.
- The debt taxonomy (life, management, financial, technological) is presented as a portfolio-level strategy - he says his later companies grew faster because he deliberately took financial debt to avoid the other kinds.

## I Built a Business That Runs Without Me
`EdUAAVKl_io` · https://youtu.be/EdUAAVKl_io

*The jump from rich (doing the right things) to wealthy (owning the right things) is made by delegating not only the job you do now but the next job you would have learned - hire someone who already has that skill and keep only ownership.*

### Case study

**Business.** Hormozi's own two fitness businesses inside the Acquisition.com portfolio - the worked example of active income becoming ownership income

**Starting state.** 2018: $17M profit from the two businesses, with him doing everything in them

**Diagnosis.** He was getting paid for what he did, not for what he owned. The degree to which he had personally grown the business capped it, because he only ever delegated one step ahead and kept learning the next skill himself.

**Intervention.**

- Delegate the job you are currently doing to someone else
- Instead of learning the next job yourself, find who already has that skill and give them the next job too (jump two steps)
- Interview candidates to learn the role and hire people who know more than you do
- Keep ownership of the business rather than doing the work inside it

**Result.** He now does nothing in those two businesses. Profit went from $17M (2018) to a projected $8.1M this year with COVID for half the year, yet the businesses are 'more valuable now' than when he did everything. On a $8M base, a 20% return means going to $9.6M next year, which he calls ridiculously easy now that he can assemble rather than build.

**Numbers.**

| Claim | Value |
|---|---|
| Profit from the two fitness businesses in 2018 | **$17 million** |
| Expected profit for the same two businesses this year | **$8.1 million** |
| Return target and the growth required | **20% / $8 million to $9.6 million** |

> **Transferable pattern.** Do not delegate one step at a time. At each step, hand over both the job you do now AND the next job you would have learned, to someone who already has that skill. You get the same growth curve the business had when you ran it, but you keep only the ownership. Corollary test: if you are the one doing the highest-value work in the business, you own a job, not a business - and your own skill level caps the business at the size you can personally run.

**Where this corrects or extends the written summaries.**

- Generic 'who not how' summaries say to delegate what you do; Hormozi's version here goes one step further - delegate the NEXT job too, to someone who already has the skill - and he prices the cost of getting it wrong with real numbers ($17M profit in 2018 with him operating vs $8.1M projected with…
- The claim that a business is worth MORE when the owner is absent is asserted without a valuation multiple, only by the profit swing and his stated 20% return requirement.

## I Blew Up A Secret Business To Prove It's Not Luck
`SmiOK8Yun4s` · https://youtu.be/SmiOK8Yun4s

*Teardown of a fashion stylist doing $309K a year: fix the leaky funnel (lead magnet, VSSL, harder qualification, headline), scale the affiliate channel she was ignoring (6 partners already produced a third of revenue), and reprice with a high-end anchor and a default back-end.*

### Case study

**Business.** Ashley's fashion styling business - personal styling and design advice for high-income clients (CEOs, socialites, frequent flyers) with a $8,500 60-day onboarding, continuity, and 10% affiliate commission on clothing

**Starting state.** $309,000 revenue and $130,000 profit last year (42% margins), over 1,000 lifetime customers. Paid cold traffic went to a form with no headline, no value proposition and a 'get more info' button: 1,800 clicks produced 8 sales. No lead magnet, no video sales letter, no qualification. Six affiliates (found by a basic Google search and 40 emails) produced a third of the business. Most clients were grandfathered at $1,500/quarter or less, only a couple paid the $2,000/month tier, and no pricing was…

**Diagnosis.** Three constraints. (1) A leaky funnel - the bottleneck is conversion, not traffic, and it leaks because there is no reason to opt in and no qualification. (2) A missed gold mine - a channel already proven to work (6 affiliates, one-third of revenue, $2,500 of revenue per email sent) was being run at almost zero volume. (3) Pricing - no price anchor, no frictionless upsell from onboarding into continuity, and a premium brand delivered through a commodity sales process, so the frame breaks and conversion drops. He also places her at stage four of the scaling roadmap: identify and double down on…

**Intervention.**

- Funnel, step 1: add a lead magnet - a personalized style assessment (skin tone, body type, lifestyle) qualified on BANT plus income, since it is high-touch concierge work, not an ebook
- Funnel, step 2: install a VSSL - a 3-7 minute video that pre-frames the assessment and pre-sells the call; built by recording answers to the most common objections from sales recordings and sending it to every prospect in the funnel
- Funnel, step 3: qualify harder - BANT plus an income question in the intake form so unqualified buyers are filtered before she creates a personalized assessment
- Funnel, step 4: rewrite the headline and subheadline around the freebie: 'free lookbook to dress for your tone using your existing closet' (the 'using your existing closet' kills the biggest objection)
- Affiliates, step 1: build the list beyond matchmakers - private social clubs, executive coaches, high-end personal trainers, divorce attorneys - anyone whose customers are in the buying moment (recently divorced, newly single executives, wives whose husbands' dress does not match their status)
- Affiliates, step 2: send the emails - 400 in 30 days, 20 per day, no fancy software
- Affiliates, step 3: activate and retain - treat affiliates like customers, farm the relationship (dinners, events hosted for their customers, sponsorship that makes the affiliate look good)
- Pricing, step 1: introduce a highest-tier continuity option (about 10x) so the $2,000/month and $4,500/quarter look reasonable, keeping the anchor similar to the core offer with only one or two differences nobody cares about
- Pricing, step 2: make the back-end the default next step after onboarding ('you have already invested in your transformation, let's make sure it sticks')
- Pricing, step 3: keep pushing price until the conversion drop costs more than the price gain - the point of maximum profit is after the point of maximum conversion

**Result.** No measured results are given in this video - the fixes are presented as a plan. His estimates: the funnel rebuild (lead magnet, VSSL, qualification, headline) could give her 2-3x, and if only 10% of clients take a high-end anchor priced at 10x, it effectively doubles the revenue of the business.

| Affiliate concentration | **six partners send a third of her entire business** |
| Affiliate expansion goal | **40 emails to date to 400 emails in 30 days (20/day), 6…** |
| Price anchor effect | **10% take at 10x price doubles revenue** |
| Estimated funnel fix effect | **2-3x** |

> **Transferable pattern.** Three moves, in this order. (1) Before buying more traffic, stop the leak: give a narrow, qualified free thing, add a short pre-call video built from recorded objections, and qualify with BANT so unqualified people never consume your delivery time. (2) Hunt the gold mine already inside the business - find the channel that produced outsized results and do 10x more of it (here: 40 emails had produced 6 partners worth a third of revenue, so the instruction was 400 emails in 30 days, plus a relationship owner to keep partners activated). ## I Doubled A Business in 60 Days to Show It's Not Luck (Part 1)

**Business.** His own portfolio (Acquisition.com and the businesses behind the '$100 million in a single niche') — self-teardown against the benchmark of seven peers in a '$500 million dollar mastermind'.
