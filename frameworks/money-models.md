# Money Models
> Public methodology redrafted for personal use from MIT/Apache-licensed sources (wowsergei/hormozi-gtm, Wrenbjor, genshin-hermes, zacker-tech). Not affiliated with or endorsed by Alex Hormozi.

## Load this when
- Pricing a new offer or repackaging an existing one.
- Revenue per customer is flat and there is no backend.
- Cash runs out before acquisition spend pays back.
- You need upsells, downsells, or a continuity layer.
- Planning a launch, promo, winback, or fast-cash sprint.
- Diagnosing payback period, churn, or unit economics.
- Building a product ladder or ascension path for an audience.
- Deciding which offer to add next, and in what order.
- A competitor sells "the same thing" but makes far more per customer.
- LTV:CAC or payback period looks unhealthy on a dashboard.
- A launch underperformed and you need to know which layer to fix first.
- You are choosing between raising prices, adding a bonus, or adding a backend offer.

## Core frameworks

### 1. The money model stack (attraction → core → upsell → downsell → continuity)

A money model is the deliberate sequence of offers you make: what you sell, in what order, at what price, and at what moment. Optimize the sequence, not a single offer. Five positions:

1. **Attraction** - turns a stranger into a first-time buyer. Aim it at the biggest barrier to the first purchase: price, trust, time, or risk.
2. **Core** - the main thing the buyer came for. The attraction offer leads directly into it.
3. **Upsell** - whatever you offer right after the core purchase, while attention and trust peak. Most profit usually lives here.
4. **Downsell** - the alternate path for every no. Never the same product cheaper; change how they pay or what they get.
5. **Continuity** - the recurring layer that keeps revenue turning after the transaction ends. Build it last.

Map the whole catalog on the Value Grid - price on one axis (low → high), purchase frequency on the other (one-time → recurring):

|  | One-time | Recurring |
|---|---|---|
| **Low price** | Tripwire / attraction | Entry continuity |
| **High price** | High-ticket core | Premium continuity |

Fill empty quadrants in order of ease, not order of excitement. Every layer should hand the buyer a clear next step.

Two numbers decide whether the stack works: cash collected within 30 days per customer, and gross profit per customer over the full relationship. Track both after every offer you add. If an addition raises lifetime value but slows 30-day cash below the payback line, it belongs later in the sequence than you placed it.

### 2. Attraction offers (5 types)

Free and discounted are points on one continuum, not opposites. Free pulls volume; discounts pull higher show-up rates. Choose by the barrier that blocks the first purchase.

1. **Win Your Money Back** - The buyer pays, then earns a refund by hitting stated criteria: do X in Y within Z, under clear rules. Qualify by results, actions, or both. Criteria must be easy to track, tied to a real result, and worth advertising. Profit comes from the winners who keep buying - this fails without an upsell ready behind it. Deliver refunds as store credit drawn down as a monthly discount, never as free months. Only run it when your refund rate sits under 5%.
2. **Giveaway** - Run an entry contest where the grand prize is the exact product you want everyone to buy. One winner takes it; every other entrant gets a time-limited promotional offer. Value-anchor the prize, create urgency at three points (entering, claiming, using), and keep the discount within 10-30% of gross margin. Queue downsells for non-winners. If it flops, the prize wasn't grand enough. Check sweepstakes law - this type is regulated.
3. **Decoy** - Advertise a stripped or cheaper version, then place it beside a premium option. Present the premium first, make the contrast dramatic, sell outcomes instead of feature lists, and ask whether they came for the free thing or the lasting result. Discount offers show better attendance than free offers - use them where no-shows are expensive. Test four ways to state the discount: percent off, dollars off, free portion, total package.
4. **Buy X Get Y Free** - Turn a discount into a bundle giveaway by selling multiples. Raise the base price before you give anything away. Give away more units than they pay for; several cheap free items can beat one expensive one. Lengthens the commitment window and pulls prepaid cash forward. Cap it near 10% of customers when using it purely for a cash pop.
5. **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** - Offer a choice: full price later (delayed payment, conditional guarantee, card on file) or less today (20-50% off plus bonuses). Removes price risk at the decision moment and lets you advertise without fear. If more than 10% of the pay-later group cancels, the offer was too weak.

Match the type to the barrier: cash risk → Pay Less Now; trust → Win Your Money Back; attention → Giveaway; sticker shock → Decoy or Buy X Get Y.

Before committing, grade each candidate attraction offer on four questions: does it pull the target buyer specifically, does it leave enough cash to fund 2+ future acquisitions, does it have an obvious next purchase to hand into, and can you operate it without heroics? Two no answers means pick a different type. A pure free-goodwill offer (no cash, no next step) is a lead magnet, not an attraction offer - don't confuse the two.

### 3. Upsells (4 types)

Upsells solve the customer's next problem the moment it appears - after purchase, before momentum dies. They fail three ways: wrong thing, wrong time, wrong delivery. Always feature the higher-profit option first.

1. **Classic** - Solve the next immediate problem while the buying window is open. Surprise and delight, then present the option. Ask for the no early so a yes is possible (say-no-to-say-yes). Exploit the Hyper Buying Cycle: the short window right after purchase when appetites peak. Book the next meeting before ending the current one (BAMFAM). Present three price options to nudge buyers to the middle.
2. **Menu** - Offer options instead of one upsell. Four moves: Unselling (name what they don't need), Prescription (name what they need and why), A/B choice (which one, not whether), card-on-file capture so every later yes takes one step.
3. **Anchor** - Show a premium option at 5-10× the main price first, let the gasp land, then rescue them with the main offer, which now reads as cheap. Some buyers take the premium anyway - always keep one ready for budgets that can absorb it.
4. **Rollover** - Credit what they already paid toward a bigger next offer. Rule: the next offer must be at least 4× the credit, keeping the effective discount at 25% or less. Four audiences: lapsed, upset, a competitor's customers, current customers. Pair with a winback campaign, one-time urgency, and a gift-card play (gift card at roughly 20% of the next offer's price).

### 4. Downsells (3 types)

A downsell is a trade, not a pressure play. Personalize it. Never repeat the same offer at a lower price - that trains buyers to wait you out.

1. **Payment Plan** - Same product, same total price, spread over time. Work down this ladder in order:
   - Reward for paying in full (the default frame).
   - Third-party financing, alternate card, or layaway.
   - Half now, half later.
   - Want-check: "1-10, how much do you want this?" - proceed at 8 or higher.
   - One-third down plus two payments.
   - Evenly spread payments.
   - Trial.
   Align due dates to paychecks, re-run declined cards, and protect the share of buyers paying in full. Cadence shifts churn: roughly 10.7% monthly, 5% quarterly, 2% annual - start people on longer cycles when cash allows.
2. **Trial With Penalty** - They start free if they meet the terms; they pay only if they don't. Mirror the Win Your Money Back criteria. Take the card first, then explain the penalty. Keep scenario-based upsells ready: loved it / hated it / never used it.
3. **Feature Downsell** - Remove features to lower the price. Remove highest-value features first to push them back to the original; remove low-value features with a steep price drop to make the downgrade land. Name the combinations (aspirational top tier, and a clearly labeled minimum at the bottom). Accept reviews, testimonials, or referrals as partial payment when cash is the blocker.

### 5. Continuity offers (3 types)

Build continuity last, after the one-time offers work. You are stacking three levers: bonuses, discounts, and fees.

1. **Continuity Bonus** - Add a bonus for joining today; it must be worth more than the first payment. Advertise the bonus, not the membership. The standalone-to-continuity price ratio steers the mix:

   | Ratio | % choosing continuity |
   |---|---|
   | 1.33× | ~50% |
   | 1.66× | ~60% |
   | 2× | ~70% |
   | 2.33× | ~80% |
   | 2.66× | ~90% |

   Add a bulk-prepaid upsell (for example, 10 months for the price of 12) - it lifts 30-day profit sharply even at a one-in-eight take rate.
2. **Continuity Discount** - Trade free time for commitment. Apply it up front, at the end, spread across months, or after the first one or two payments. Bill every four weeks instead of monthly: 13 cycles a year instead of 12 adds about 8.3% revenue at the same listed price. Add a 3% processing fee. Capture a second payment method (ACH) so card failures don't churn customers.
3. **Waived Fee** - Charge a setup fee of 3-5× the monthly rate, then waive it in return for a term commitment; leaving early makes the fee due. People stay longer when leaving costs more than staying. If over 5% cancel early, fix onboarding and delivery before adding more acquisition.

### 6. Client-Financed Acquisition (30-day cash rule; the math)

Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) means the customer funds their own acquisition - fast. The 30-day rule: earn more gross profit from a customer inside 30 days than it costs to acquire and deliver to them. One customer should fund acquiring and servicing at least two more. Run the cycle monthly: it works like an interest-free 30-day credit line you can recycle forever.

Five CFA levels:
- Level 1: recovered in the first transaction.
- Level 2: recovered within 30 days.
- Level 3: recovered within 90 days.
- Level 4: recovered within 12 months.
- Level 5: never recovered - that is a hobby, not a business.

Run this math per customer:
```
30-day gross profit =
  (core price − delivery cost)
  + (upsell gross profit × take rate)
  + (continuity revenue in month 1 − delivery × take rate)

Payback check:   30-day gross profit ≥ CAC + delivery cost
CFA target:      30-day gross profit ≥ 2 × (CAC + delivery cost)  → funds two more
LTGP:CAC         = lifetime gross profit ÷ CAC
```

Speed compounds. Double value per customer, double volume, and double cycle speed → 8× growth. Triple all three → 27×.

When the 30-day check fails, fix in this order:
1. Add or move an upsell to the point of greatest need - fastest lift, no new delivery.
2. Raise the core price in stages until profit peaks.
3. Switch continuity to 4-week billing and add a prepaid option.
4. Cut delivery cost with group or digital formats.
5. Only then attack CAC with new creative or channels - acquisition is the slowest lever to move.

### 7. Sequencing and rollover rules

Three stages, each funded by the one before:
- **Stage I - Get cash:** install the attraction offer. Get customers predictably.
- **Stage II - Get more cash:** add upsells and downsells. Make customers pay for themselves.
- **Stage III - Get the most cash:** add continuity. Make customers pay for other customers, then spend maximum on acquisition.

When adding any offer, check four things: right stage (attraction acquires, upsell lifts order value, continuity retains), right problem (solvable with resources you already have), right delivery (solved the way the customer wants it solved), right timing (they are hungry for this now).

Operating rules:
- Build one offer at a time; never launch the whole model in one pass.
- Put continuity last, then a bulk-prepaid upsell that rolls into recurring.
- Roll credit forward only when the next offer is worth at least 4× the credit.
- Raise prices in stages until profit peaks; start high and work down, never the reverse.
- After a no, never offer the same thing cheaper - change the how or the what.
- BAMFAM on every call that doesn't close.
- Fill catalog gaps with affiliate products before building something new.
- Keep it simple: many offers can run on one product. Delivery complexity is what kills execution.

### 8. Fast cash campaigns (when and how)

Run a fast-cash campaign when money is needed now: bridging payroll, funding a new ad channel, covering a seasonal dip, or validating a new offer before committing.

Sequence the launch:
- 7 days out: tease something big.
- 5 days out: announcement plus core offer details.
- 3 days out: reveal bonus one.
- 2 days out: bonus two plus scarcity.
- 1 day out: final bonus plus urgency.
- Day of: open the cart with a countdown.
- Then 24 / 12 / 6 / 1 hours out: reminder, warning, last chance, cart closing.

Tools that stack cash quickly:
- Prepaid annual or bulk offers (10 months for 12).
- Bonus stacks with real deadlines - bonuses expire, price rises.
- Rollover winback to lapsed customers and their stored credit.
- Announced price increases with a grandfather window for existing buyers.
- Affiliate promotions for adjacent products.

Small campaign math: 10 buyers × $6,000 = $60,000, plus 3 new continuity customers. Run the payback check on the campaign's own spend before calling it a win.

## Decision rules & thresholds
- **30-day rule:** 30-day gross profit per customer covers CAC + delivery. Target: one customer funds 2+ more (30-day GP ≥ 2× unit cost).
- **LTGP:CAC:** 3:1 or better. Below that, every sale digs a deeper hole.
- **Payback windows:** 30 days = CFA target; 90 days = acceptable ceiling; 12 months = weak; beyond 12 months = red line, fix the model or cut spend.
- **Rollover:** next offer value ≥ 4× the credit; effective discount ≤ 25%.
- **Anchor:** premium at 5-10× the main price, presented before the main offer.
- **Giveaway discount:** 10-30% of gross margin. Never deeper.
- **Win Your Money Back refunds:** under 5%; above that the criteria or the product is broken.
- **Pay-later cancellations:** 10% or less; more means the offer is too weak.
- **Waived-fee early cancellations:** under 5%; above that, investigate delivery.
- **Want-check:** proceed at 8+ on a 1-10 scale; below, present the downsell.
- **Continuity mix:** standalone-to-continuity ratio between 1.33× and 2.66× yields 50-90% continuity selection.
- **Billing:** 4-week cycles (13 per year, +8.3% revenue), 3% processing fee, secondary payment method captured.
- **Churn by cadence:** monthly ≈ 10.7%, quarterly ≈ 5%, annual ≈ 2%. Move customers to longer cycles and prepay.
- **Churn math:** 15% monthly churn erases about 83% of customers in a year. Halving churn roughly doubles average lifespan and LTV.
- **Price raises:** losing fewer than 20% of customers means the increase was profitable. Announce in advance; grandfather existing buyers for 3-6 months.
- **Value-to-price:** aim for 10:1 perceived value against price.
- **Margin targets:** digital 90%+, group programs 70-85%, productized services 50-70%, custom services 30-50%. Service gross margin floor: 70%.
- **Net revenue retention:** above 100% - existing customers should spend more over time.
- **Prepaid promos:** cap Buy X Get Y and similar at ~10% of the customer base when used for a cash pop.
- **Front end:** break even or take a small profit on the first sale. Never plan to lose money on every customer and hope the backend rescues you.
- **AOV lift:** a working upsell layer raises average order value 2× or more. Below that, rework the timing or the offer itself.
- **Payment plans:** price the plan at a small premium over pay-in-full (for example, full price up front, or six installments at a higher total) so paying in full reads as the reward.
- **High-ticket backend:** keep one high-ticket offer ($5K+) available even at low take rates; it raises expected value per customer materially.
- **Red lines:** never discount the same product to close; never run continuity without an easy cancel path; never explain trial penalties before taking the card; never deploy the full stack at once.

## Build checklist
- [ ] Map current revenue streams with monthly revenue, margin, and growth rate each.
- [ ] Compute CAC, AOV, purchase frequency, lifespan, LTV, and LTV:CAC.
- [ ] Verify 30-day gross profit covers CAC + delivery, or name exactly what is missing.
- [ ] Pick one attraction offer type based on the biggest first-purchase barrier.
- [ ] Confirm the attraction offer leaves enough cash to fund 2+ future acquisitions.
- [ ] Add one upsell at the customer's next problem; higher-profit option first.
- [ ] Add one anchor (5-10×) for buyers with real budget.
- [ ] Add one downsell that changes how they pay or what they get - never the same thing cheaper.
- [ ] Add continuity last, with a bonus worth more than the first payment.
- [ ] Switch billing to 4-week cycles; add processing fee and backup payment method.
- [ ] Add a bulk-prepaid or annual option that rolls into recurring.
- [ ] Perfect one offer at a time; do not ship the full model in one pass.
- [ ] Raise prices in stages; test on new customers before rolling out.
- [ ] Re-run the 30-day payback check after every addition.
- [ ] Keep the offer count small and the product single - complexity is a cost.

## Audit checklist
- [ ] No upsell after checkout → most of the available profit is missing.
- [ ] No continuity → revenue resets to zero every month; add it last, but add it.
- [ ] No downsell path → every no walks away; capture with payment terms or a feature tier.
- [ ] Payback over 90 days → raise front-end gross profit or cut CAC.
- [ ] 30-day gross profit below CAC → the model grows you into insolvency; fix before scaling spend.
- [ ] Margin leakage → audit delivery costs, discounts, refunds, and processing fees line by line.
- [ ] Discounting the same product to close → rebuild the offer, not the price.
- [ ] Continuity used as the attraction offer → resequence; it belongs at the end.
- [ ] Fake anchor nobody buys → remove it or make it a real option.
- [ ] Bonus values that don't hold up → unbelievable values discredit the entire stack.
- [ ] Hard-to-cancel process → silent churn, chargebacks, and one-star reviews; make cancel easy and ask why.
- [ ] Refund, cancel, or early-cancel rates above thresholds → fix criteria, onboarding, or delivery.
- [ ] Prepaid cash treated as profit → reserve it against delivery obligations.
- [ ] Attraction offer converts but leaves no surplus cash → payback problem hiding behind a healthy lead flow.
- [ ] No fast-cash option available when cash is tight → review prepaid, annual, and winback plays before the next crunch.
- [ ] Model copied from a competitor → their unit economics are not yours.

## Common mistakes
- Running only a front end: no upsell, no downsell, no continuity.
- Measuring payback in years instead of covering acquisition inside 30 days.
- Discounting the same product to rescue a close.
- Cutting price out of fear instead of adding bonuses, guarantees, or terms.
- Giving away free product without raising the base price first.
- Treating prepaid cash as earned revenue instead of an obligation.
- Inflating bonus values until buyers distrust the offer.
- Using continuity as the attraction instead of the retention layer.
- Launching every offer at once, then not knowing which one moves numbers.
- Explaining trial penalties before collecting the card.
- Making cancellation hard - silent churn costs more than honest churn.
- Expecting Win Your Money Back losers to be the profit source; the money comes from winners who stay and buy more.

## Output contract
When asked to design a money model, produce sections: current-state unit economics; the five-position stack (attraction, core, upsell, downsell, continuity) with prices and take-rate assumptions; 30-day payback calculation; build sequence; fast-cash option when cash is tight; risks and thresholds. Save artifact as MONEY_MODEL.md.

## Short examples
- **30-day payback check.** Sell at $500 with $50 delivery cost → $450 gross profit. CAC $200, so unit cost is $250. Self-funding covers $250; the leftover $200 funds 0.8 of the next customer - short of the 2× target ($500). Add one upsell: $400 gross profit at 25% take = $100 expected. New 30-day gross profit = $550 vs required $500 → one customer funds two more, with $50 buffer. Green.
- **Adding continuity.** Sell a $299 one-time product. Add a $129/month membership; ratio 2.32× → roughly 80% pick continuity. Expected first-month value per buyer = 0.8 × $129 + 0.2 × $299 = $163. At 7% monthly churn the average life is ~14 months → continuity LTV ≈ $1,806 per joiner against a $299 one-time sale. Add a first-payment-beating bonus to lift the take rate further.
- **Upsell sequencing.** After a core purchase: (a) implementation add-on, $400, 25% take → +$100 expected; (b) premium anchor, $4,000, 5% take → +$200 expected; (c) payment-plan downsell for the nos; (d) continuity bonus at checkout. The sequence adds $300 expected gross profit per buyer before the downsell and continuity are counted. The rental-car pattern shows the mechanics: a base rental of roughly $19/day climbed toward $100/day through four sequential offers, each solving a different live problem (bigger car, flexible return, damage cover, prepaid fuel).
- **Fast-cash sprint.** You have 2,000 past buyers. Offer a $1,500 annual prepay with an expiring bonus stack. At 3% conversion: 60 buyers × $1,500 = $90,000 collected in one week. Deliveries now sit against prepaid obligations, so ring-fence the cash for service costs. Run it over owned channels (email, SMS) so the campaign clears the payback check on margin, and expire the bonuses on a hard deadline.
